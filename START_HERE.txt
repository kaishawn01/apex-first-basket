APEX V2.44.0 — Recent opening-shot role inputs

After Run rankings, expand Recent opening-shot role · prediction preview. This new experimental forecast uses each selected player's last five recorded starts on the same team, retaining only starts within 60 days before the prediction date. At least three of these starts must have an observed team opening shooter to activate the input. Unknown opening shooters do not count as misses, and the engine does not search farther back to replace unknown starts within the last-five set. These are recorded starts in the saved history, not a claim of complete appearance coverage.

The recent-role opening-shot rate is (recent opening shots + 10 × older anchor rate) / (recent observed starts + 10). Recent observations receive equal weight. The older anchor uses existing smoothed Recent-form opening-shot estimates with 90-day decay, excluding that player's recent-window starts so they are not counted twice. All evidence is same-team, strictly earlier-date and within 400 days. Without three recent observations, the player's original Recent-form shot rate is retained. With no active players on a team, the forecast exactly falls back to Recent form. An inactive player's final probability can still move when active teammates change the team normalization.

The adjusted shot rate enters the existing 65/35 shot/conditional-first-score blend. Conditional scoring history and team-first probabilities retain Recent-form behavior. The preview shows observed counts, raw recent rate, older anchor, adjusted rate and each player's probability change against Recent form. It uses the same selected five and is hidden when ranking inputs change. A CSV export provides the input evidence and estimates. No additional API calls are required.

It includes approximate paired date-bootstrap intervals and per-game CSV export, with stale-history/training-setting guards. Fallback games are retained. The five-start window, 60-day cap, three-observation minimum and strength-10 anchor are experimental settings, not fitted or validated accuracy improvements. Historical observed starters, incomplete history, nonstarter-first-scorer exclusions and repeated comparisons remain limitations.

This is a separate prediction preview and research challenger. Main rankings, odds calculations, the five-model scorecard and forward-journal model bundles retain their existing behavior. APEX V2.39.0 — Post-miss scoring challenger

New imports and selected-game enrichment record a game-level opening_score_path: free_throw_before_fg, opening_fg_made, opening_fg_missed, or blank when the sequence cannot be established. The path is shared across all ten starter rows and audited for consistency. A free throw scored after the opening field-goal miss belongs to the post-miss path. Missing shot outcomes are not inferred from a later scorer. ESPN's named-player wording such as Player misses is now recognized with explicit non-scoring, zero-point shot metadata; previously these misses remained unknown. Enrichment can add the missing outcomes and paths without changing existing scoring facts, with backups and concurrency protection.

The experimental post_miss model has three probability branches. It estimates an early-free-throw chance from relevant known earlier games with a 1-in-20 prior. On the remaining branch, opening-team rates use observed game-opening shooters with a 50/50, strength-20 prior and the existing bounded matchup comparison. Within-team shot shares come from recent opening-shot frequencies; make probabilities use the conversion model's shrunk rates. Made-attempt mass goes directly to each shooter. The remaining miss mass is distributed using first-scorer outcomes from earlier opening-miss games. Early-free-throw mass uses its own conditional outcome distribution. Each recovery distribution has a strength-10 conversion-model anchor trained on games outside that path, so context games do not also train their anchor. The final ten probabilities are normalized.

Both teams need at least ten earlier games with known paths; otherwise the model exactly reproduces Opening conversion. These are review minimums and experimental priors, not validated reliability thresholds or measured league averages. All evidence is earlier-date only, team-specific for players, inside 400 days with 90-day decay. Unknown or nonstarter game-opening shooters do not identify the opening team in the opening-team estimator. The model learns eventual recovery outcomes rather than explicitly simulating rebounds, turnovers or individual later possessions.

Backtest lab adds Test post-miss scoring model, comparing against Opening conversion on identical games. All-date, newest-quarter and after-sequence-minimum sections separate active modeling from fallback. Path counts, error and fractional hit metrics, approximate paired date-bootstrap intervals and CSV exports are available. Repeated testing, sparse paths, observed historical starters and the nonstarter-first-scorer exclusion remain limitations. Live defaults and journal forecasts are unchanged pending validation.

The experimental conversion model starts with Recent form's team probabilities, opening-shot frequency and conditional first-score signal. For each selected player, q = (weighted opening makes + 5) / (weighted known opening attempts + 10). The raw player weight is 0.65 * opening-shot rate * (q / 0.5) + 0.35 * conditional first-score rate, then normalized within the team. Evidence is team-specific, earlier-date only, within 400 days and decayed with a 90-day half-life. The neutral 50% prior and ten-attempt strength are experimental choices, not measured league averages. Unknown outcomes never enter conversion denominators. With no conversion evidence, the model reproduces Recent form. Conversion can change player order.
